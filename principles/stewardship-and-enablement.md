# Leave people and systems more capable

**Strength:** Principle

## The problem

A platform team can solve a problem in a way that makes others rely on the team itself, rather than on a capability they can use on their own. It may fulfill the same request by hand each time, hold knowledge that is not written down, or build a capability that only its authors can change safely. That approach can produce a result quickly. It also means the client and the delivery team cannot make progress when the platform team is unavailable, and they are left without the knowledge they need when the engagement ends.

## The position

The team takes responsibility for leaving the systems and the people it works with in better condition than it found them. Working correctly is the first requirement for a capability, but it is not the last. A capability is complete when the people who use it can understand it, operate it, and change it with confidence, without needing its authors to be present. Knowledge the team gains during an engagement belongs where others can find and use it.

This serves the client's long-term interest. Clients often operate systems after an engagement ends, and delivery teams change over time. Capabilities that are documented, tested, and understandable remain useful after the people who built them have moved to other work.

## What the team expects

- Prefer a lasting capability to a task the team repeats by hand. When the team fulfills the same request several times, consider whether documentation, a self-service option, or automation would let the requesting team meet the need without waiting for the platform team.
- Explain recommendations, including their benefits, cost, risks, and maintenance burden, so the client can make an informed decision and maintain the result.
- Record what the team learns in the repository or document that owns the subject, so the next engineer does not need to rediscover it.
- Ask for help at the edge of current knowledge, and share what was learned with the project and, where useful, with the wider practice. **Asking for help is an expected part of professional work.**
- Design for the whole life of a capability, including upgrades, configuration changes, recovery, replacement, and retirement, as well as its first deployment.
